### 7. DIGITAL FORENSICS LAB

**Page Heading:**
> Digital Forensics Lab

**Introduction:**
> The Digital Forensics Lab documents my practical forensic work in controlled and authorized environments. It serves as a technical record of laboratory exercises, investigation scenarios, evidence examination, tool usage, findings, and forensic documentation.

**Lab Categories:**

**Evidence Acquisition**
> Practical exercises involving the acquisition and preservation of digital evidence while maintaining evidence integrity and documenting the acquisition process.

**Disk & File-System Forensics**
> Examination of storage media, partitions, file systems, deleted data, file metadata, system artifacts, and user activity.

**Memory Forensics**
> Examination of volatile memory to identify processes, network connections, loaded modules, user activity, and other relevant artifacts.

**Timeline Analysis**
> Reconstruction of system and user activity by correlating timestamps and artifacts from multiple evidence sources.

**Artifact Analysis**
> Examination of operating-system, application, browser, user, and system artifacts to identify information relevant to an investigation.

**Forensic Reporting**
> Documentation of forensic procedures, observations, findings, supporting evidence, limitations, and conclusions.

**Lab Principle:**
> Every examination should be reproducible, documented, and grounded in the available evidence.

## Content Insertion Guidelines

**Critical Rule:** Only insert factual information you actually possess.

For the following sections, create professional wording now but fill actual information from your records:

- **Certifications & Education:** Names, institutions, dates, credential IDs
- **Projects:** Project titles, objectives, scenarios, tools, findings, conclusions
- **Lab Exercises:** Specific exercises, methodology, findings
- **Research & Notes:** Actual research topics, technical notes, lessons learned
- **Contact Information:** Real email, LinkedIn profile, CV location
- **Education & Training:** Actual programmes, courses, providers, years

**Tone:** Professional, precise, evidence-based. Avoid generic language, marketing speak, or unfounded claims.

---

## Important Notes on Credibility

1. **No Invented Information:** Every fact, achievement, certification, and project should be verifiable or drawn from your actual experience.
2. **Distinction of Experience Levels:** Use language that accurately reflects your level (e.g., "developing knowledge," "practical experience," "learning") rather than claiming expertise where it doesn't exist.
3. **Evidence Disclaimer:** Include the disclaimer on the Case Studies page that all materials are from authorized, fictional, or publicly available sources.
4. **Professional Accuracy:** Forensics is a regulated field. Ensure all terminology, processes, and claims align with industry standards.
